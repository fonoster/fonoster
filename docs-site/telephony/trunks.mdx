---
title: "Connect a SIP trunk"
description: "Connect any SIP carrier to Fonoster with a trunk, so its numbers ring your agents and your calls go out through it."
---

A trunk is your connection to a carrier. Inbound, the carrier sends calls for your numbers to
Fonoster. Outbound, Fonoster sends your calls to the carrier. Use a trunk to bring numbers from any
carrier that speaks SIP. For a Twilio number or a WhatsApp Business number,
[Link a Twilio number](/telephony/link-twilio) and
[Link a WhatsApp Business number](/telephony/link-whatsapp) do all of this for you.

## Before you begin

Ask your carrier for:

- **How they authenticate you**: by IP address, by username and password, or both.
- **Where to send outbound calls**: a host name or IP, a port and a transport (UDP, TCP or TLS).
- **The addresses they send calls from**, if you want to accept calls from those addresses only.
- **The format of your numbers** in the calls they send, and the format they expect in the calls
  you send them.

## Find your connection details

Your carrier needs to know where to send calls and which addresses to allow. The trunk form in the
dashboard shows both, under **Inbound URI**:

> Point your carrier at `<host>:<port>` (`<transport>`); allowlist `<addresses>`.

Give your carrier the SIP address, and ask them to allowlist the listed addresses if they filter by
IP. If your workspace runs in a [private region](/private-regions/overview), the region's own
addresses are under **Settings** → **Region**, in **Connection details**.

## Create the trunk

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add credentials (if your carrier uses them)">
    If your carrier gave you a username and password, save them as credentials first. Fonoster uses
    them to sign in when it sends calls to the carrier.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Dashboard">
        Go to **Credentials**, choose **Add credentials**, and enter a **Name**, the **Username**
        and the **Password**. Passwords are never shown again.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="CLI">
        ```bash
        fonoster sipnet:credentials:create
        ```
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

    Usernames take lowercase letters, numbers, dots, underscores and hyphens.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add an access control list (optional)">
    An access control list (ACL) is an allowlist of IP addresses. Attach one to the trunk so only
    your carrier's addresses can send calls in. Anything not listed is rejected.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Dashboard">
        Go to **ACLs**, choose **Add ACL**, name it (for example `Acme signaling IPs`), and add
        each address or range, such as `200.88.12.0/24`.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="CLI">
        The command asks for a name and a comma-separated list of IPv4 addresses or CIDR ranges:

        ```bash
        fonoster sipnet:acls:create
        ```
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

  </Step>

  <Step title="Create the trunk">
    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Dashboard">
        Go to **Trunks** and choose **Add trunk**. The form has four parts.

        **Inbound · carrier → Fonoster**

        - **Name**: the carrier's name, such as `Acme`.
        - **Inbound URI**: a short label that identifies this trunk, such as `acme`. The form
          adds your install's `.pstn.<domain>`, so the URI reads `acme.pstn.fonoster.com`. Give it
          to your carrier: they send calls for your numbers to this host. It must be unique, so if
          it's taken, pick another.

        **Outbound · Fonoster → carrier**

        Add a row for each address your carrier gave you, with its **Host**, **Port** and
        **Transport**. Fill in **User** only if your carrier asks for one. Fonoster tries enabled
        URIs by **Priority**, then spreads calls by **Weight**. A trunk without outbound URIs
        takes calls in but can't send them out.

        **Number format on outbound calls**

        Change these only if your carrier asks. **Called number** (the number being dialed) and
        **Caller number** (your number, shown as caller ID) each take **Leave as is**, **+E.164**,
        **E.164 without +**, **National** or **International**. After the last three, a prefix
        field takes digits, `*` or `#` to add in front. See
        [Number formats on outbound calls](#number-formats-on-outbound-calls).

        **Security**

        - **Access control list**: the ACL from the previous step.
        - **Inbound credentials**: credentials your carrier must sign in with when it sends you
          calls. Use them if your carrier authenticates by password instead of IP.
        - **Outbound credentials**: the credentials from the first step.
        - **Send REGISTER**: turn on only if your carrier requires registration.

        Choose **Create trunk**.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="SDK">
        This script creates a trunk with one outbound URI and the carrier's credentials:

        ```javascript create-trunk.js
        const SDK = require("@fonoster/sdk");

        async function main() {
          const client = new SDK.Client({
            accessKeyId: "WO7d2kq9x4m1c8b3n6p0r5t2v9y4z7a1e3"
          });
          await client.loginWithApiKey(
            process.env.FONOSTER_API_KEY,
            process.env.FONOSTER_API_SECRET
          );

          const trunks = new SDK.Trunks(client);
          const { ref } = await trunks.createTrunk({
            name: "Acme",
            inboundUri: "acme.pstn.fonoster.com", // one label under pstn.<domain>
            accessControlListRef: "8e1d4b7a-3c9f-4e2a-b6d0-7a5c1f3e9b28",
            outboundCredentialsRef: "2c6a9e4f-1b7d-4f3a-8e5c-0d9b3a7f1e64",
            sendRegister: false,
            uris: [
              {
                host: "sip.acme.com",
                port: 5060,
                transport: "UDP",
                priority: 1,
                weight: 10,
                enabled: true
              }
            ]
          });

          console.log(`Trunk created: ${ref}`);
        }

        main().catch(console.error);
        ```

        `transport` is one of `UDP`, `TCP`, `TLS`, `SCTP`, `WS` or `WSS`. Hosts are an IPv4
        address or a domain name.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="CLI">
        The command asks for the name, the inbound URI (one label under `pstn.<domain>`, such as
        `acme.pstn.fonoster.com`) and the ACL, then whether you need an
        outbound URI. If you do, it asks for the outbound credentials, host, port, transport (TCP
        or UDP) and user. Then it asks how to write the called and caller numbers on outbound
        calls (see [Number formats on outbound calls](#number-formats-on-outbound-calls)):

        ```bash
        fonoster sipnet:trunks:create
        ```

        <Warning>
          Trunks created with the CLI have **Send REGISTER** on. If your carrier doesn't expect
          registration, turn it off on the trunk in the dashboard.
        </Warning>
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your numbers">
    Add each of your carrier's numbers to the trunk in E.164 format. Fonoster also matches a number
    the carrier sends without the `+` or in the national or international form of its country; if
    the carrier sends something else, such as a label, add it to the number as an alias. Point each
    number at an agent, a stream or a SIP agent. See [Numbers and routing](/telephony/numbers).

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Configure your carrier

In your carrier's portal, set up:

- **Inbound (their origination)**: send calls for your numbers to `sip:<your inbound URI>`, such
  as `sip:acme.pstn.fonoster.com`, at the SIP address from the trunk form.
- **Authentication**: allowlist the addresses the trunk form lists, or give them the
  inbound credentials you attached to the trunk.
- **Outbound (their termination)**: accept calls from the addresses the trunk form lists,
  signed in with the outbound credentials if you set them.

Use the transport your carrier prefers, and the same one on both sides.

## Number formats on outbound calls

By default Fonoster sends numbers to your carrier as they were dialed. If your carrier asks for
another format, set it on the trunk, separately for the called number (the number being dialed)
and the caller number (your number, shown as caller ID):

| Format | The called number +442079460018, from a UK number |
| --- | --- |
| Leave as is | As dialed |
| +E.164 | `+442079460018` |
| E.164 without + | `442079460018` |
| National | `02079460018`; a number of another country is written in international form |
| International | `00442079460018`, with the international prefix of your number's country |

With **E.164 without +**, **National** and **International** you can also set a prefix, 1 to 20
digits, `*` or `#`, added in front (for example `9`). Change these only if your carrier asks.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    Go to **Trunks**, open the trunk, and under **Number format on outbound calls** choose the
    **Called number** and **Caller number** formats. After **E.164 without +**, **National** or
    **International**, enter a prefix if your carrier asks for one. Choose **Save**. Trunks made
    with Link Twilio or Link WhatsApp don't show this section.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SDK">
    ```javascript
    await trunks.updateTrunk({
      ref: "3f9a1c7e-2b4d-4e8f-a1c3-5d7e9b2f4a60",
      calledNumberFormat: "NATIONAL",
      calledNumberPrefix: "9",
      callerNumberFormat: "E164_WITHOUT_PLUS"
    });
    ```

    The formats are `NUMBER_FORMAT_UNSPECIFIED` (leave as is), `E164`, `E164_WITHOUT_PLUS`,
    `NATIONAL` and `INTERNATIONAL`. An update that leaves a field out keeps it. To go back to
    leave as is, send `NUMBER_FORMAT_UNSPECIFIED` with an empty prefix:

    ```javascript
    await trunks.updateTrunk({
      ref: "3f9a1c7e-2b4d-4e8f-a1c3-5d7e9b2f4a60",
      calledNumberFormat: "NUMBER_FORMAT_UNSPECIFIED",
      calledNumberPrefix: ""
    });
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    `fonoster sipnet:trunks:create` and `fonoster sipnet:trunks:update <ref>` ask for the called
    number format and the caller number format, and for a prefix after **E.164 without +**,
    **National** or **International**. On update, the trunk's current values are the defaults.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Check it

1. Call one of the trunk's numbers from any phone. The agent, stream or SIP agent it points at
   answers.
2. Place an outbound call from the number. See [Make outbound calls](/telephony/outbound-calls).

The trunk page lists the trunk's numbers under **Numbers on this trunk**.

If calls don't arrive:

- Check what the carrier sends for the number. Fonoster matches it in E.164, without the `+`, or in
  the national or international form of its country; for anything else, add an alias to the number.
- Check that the number is on this trunk. A number on another trunk doesn't ring.
- If the trunk has an ACL, check that it lists every address the carrier sends from.
- Check that the carrier sends to your inbound URI, not to a different host.

## Delete a trunk

Go to **Trunks**, open the trunk and choose **Delete**, or run
`fonoster sipnet:trunks:delete <ref>`. Calls to and from the carrier stop.

For every command and method, see Trunks in the [CLI reference](/reference/cli#trunks) and the
[Node SDK reference](/reference/sdk#trunks).
