### TT-B2. First replay (baseline vs a backchannel-holding candidate)

| Detector | False cut-off | Delay after true end (median / p95) | Missed barge-in | Backchannel misfire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline (VAD + 600 ms after final) | 20% | 891 / 899 ms | 0% | 100% |
| backchannel-hold (500 ms hold, lexicon) | 20% | 891 / 899 ms | 0% | 33% |

The baseline cuts off every mid-turn pause longer than about 900 ms (the long-pause calls) and
interrupts on every backchannel. The candidate passes the comparison (no regression beyond 2 points
or 100 ms) but is not made the default: a synthetic corpus is not enough evidence.

### TT-B3. Pacing handles bursts a Detector reclassifies

- A burst the Detector calls `backchannel` starts no turn and no interruption; if it ends as a
  backchannel its transcript (within 1.5 s) becomes `turn.backchannel`. If the Detector later
  calls it an interruption, the turn starts and the output is interrupted then. The baseline never
  reports backchannels, so its behaviour is unchanged.

### TT-B4. Decisions and labels in the call record

- Every turn event is recorded as `turn.decision` with the estimate (`eot`, `speaking`, `kind`) and
  `detector: baseline@1`, `policy: default@1`.
- Each turn gets a `turn.label` once its reply starts: `slow` if more than 2,000 ms passed between
  the caller's last speech and the reply, `cut-off` if the caller speaks within 1,500 ms of the
  reply starting, otherwise `ok`. Both thresholds are provisional (open question 2).
  `missed-barge-in` and `backchannel-misfire` need the truth, so they are measured by replay only.
